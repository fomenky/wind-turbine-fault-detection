📋 Business Objective

Goal: Build a drivetrain-condition classifier that reads each turbine's live SCADA sensor signature and labels it as "fault" or "normal" — enabling control-room analysts to focus on genuinely degrading machines rather than scanning the entire fleet manually.

Outcome: ✅ A Tuned XGBoost classifier trained on 30+ sensor channels that detects drivetrain faults with high recall, catching failures before they cascade into catastrophic gearbox destruction.

⚠️ Why Recall Matters: A missed fault (false negative) costs 7–21 days of lost energy revenue plus emergency crane mobilisation. A false alert (false positive) costs only a routine inspection. The model is deliberately tuned to favour catching true failures over avoiding unnecessary inspections.

Strongest Fault Indicators (Correlation with Failure):

🔧 Vibration Signatures: Drivetrain vibration RMS, bearing BPFO/BPFI frequencies, gearmesh amplitude, and sideband energy all show strong positive correlation with failure — confirming that FFT-based vibration features are the primary fault fingerprint.
🌡️ Thermal Escalation: Gearbox bearing temperature, gearbox oil temperature, and main bearing temperature are elevated in fault conditions, indicating friction-driven heat buildup as degradation progresses.
🛢️ Oil Contamination: Oil particle count is significantly higher in fault-state turbines, directly reflecting metallic debris from worn bearings and gears accumulating in the lubrication system.
📉 Power Output Drop: Faulty drivetrains show reduced power output at equivalent wind speeds, consistent with increased mechanical drag from degraded components.
📋 Maintenance History: Turbines with higher prior fault counts and longer hours since last maintenance show elevated failure rates — indicating that chronic issues and maintenance gaps compound risk.

Final Model: Tuned XGBoost

✅ Selected Model: Tuned XGBoost with scale_pos_weight for class imbalance and hyperparameters optimized via 5-fold cross-validated RandomizedSearchCV scoring on Recall.
Metric Description Business Implication
Recall (Sensitivity) Proportion of actual faults correctly detected High recall = fewer missed faults = fewer catastrophic failures
Precision Proportion of fault alerts that are genuine Higher precision = fewer unnecessary inspections dispatched
F1 Score Harmonic mean of precision and recall Balanced measure of detection quality
Accuracy Overall correct predictions Less meaningful given class imbalance — a naive model predicting "normal" achieves ~93% accuracy

Why XGBoost Was Selected:

  • Best recall on the validation set — catches the most actual faults
  • Strong generalization — minimal gap between training and validation performance
  • Native handling of class imbalance via scale_pos_weight
  • Built-in feature importance for interpretability
  • Robust to missing values and outliers in sensor data
  • Fast inference — compatible with real-time SCADA scoring at 10-minute intervals

💡 Business Insights

1. Vibration FFT features are the dominant fault signature. The model relies most heavily on frequency-domain vibration channels (BPFO, BPFI, gearmesh, sideband) — confirming that condition-based monitoring centered on spectral vibration analysis is scientifically justified for this fleet.
2. Multi-channel fusion outperforms single-threshold alarms. No single sensor channel can reliably distinguish fault from normal. The model's power comes from combining subtle, correlated shifts across vibration, thermal, lubrication, and operational channels simultaneously — exactly the pattern human analysts miss.
3. Oil contamination is an early and reliable physical marker. Oil particle count ranks among the top features. Unlike vibration (which can be masked by wind noise), oil debris is a cumulative, irreversible signal that grows monotonically as bearings degrade.
4. Maintenance history adds predictive value beyond sensor data. Prior fault count and hours since last maintenance improve detection — turbines with chronic issues or extended service gaps carry elevated baseline risk regardless of instantaneous sensor readings.
5. Failure rate is relatively uniform across hours and days. No strong time-of-day or day-of-week pattern was found, indicating that failures are mechanically driven (by load cycles and component age) rather than operationally driven by scheduling or human factors.
6. The model generalizes across the temporal split. Training on the first 70% of the timeline and testing on the final 15% confirms the classifier detects degradation patterns that persist into the future — a necessary condition for real-time deployment.

🎯 Recommendations

🚀 Immediate Deployment (0–3 months)

  • Deploy the tuned XGBoost model as a scoring service that runs on each turbine's 10-minute SCADA feed and surfaces a fault probability to the control-room dashboard
  • Set an initial alert threshold (e.g., probability > 0.5) and route flagged turbines to a priority inspection queue for field technicians
  • Instrument model predictions alongside actual outcomes to build a live validation dataset from Day 1

🔧 Maintenance Process Changes (3–6 months)

  • Shift from fixed-interval gearbox inspections to condition-triggered work orders for turbines the model flags — reducing unnecessary inspections on healthy machines
  • Pre-position crane and bearing inventory based on model alerts to convert unplanned emergency replacements into scheduled repairs at 30–50% lower cost
  • Prioritize oil sampling and vibration spot-checks on turbines with rising fault probability (0.3–0.5 range) to validate model signals before full alert

📈 Model Improvement (6–12 months)

  • Expand training data to all 1,150 turbines to capture fleet-wide variability and rare failure modes not represented in the 15-turbine pilot
  • Incorporate lead-time prediction — extend the classifier into a time-to-failure regressor so maintenance can be scheduled optimally within the remaining useful life window
  • Add rolling-window features (e.g., 24h vibration trend, 7-day oil particle slope) to capture degradation velocity, not just instantaneous state

📊 Monitoring & Governance

  • Track model drift monthly — compare predicted vs actual failure rates; retrain quarterly with fresh SCADA data to maintain detection accuracy
  • Establish a feedback loop where technicians record root-cause findings on every inspected alert (true positive vs false positive) to continuously improve precision
  • Report monthly KPIs: unplanned downtime days avoided, energy revenue protected, and mean detection lead-time (how many days before catastrophic failure the model alerted)
